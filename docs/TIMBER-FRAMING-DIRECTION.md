# Timber framing: Rye reference direction

Design direction following the user's 2026-11-02 feedback and two supplied photographs.
The wall portion is implemented in local candidate 0.1.1.110021. Manual visual acceptance remains pending.
The wider Rye palette remains deferred at the user's request.

## Diagnosis

The initial models give each one-metre cube a complete border and, optionally, an X.
Repeated cubes double the posts and rails and create a dense lattice. Timber only covers
the front and back of each oriented block, so it cannot describe a coherent outside corner.
The building needs framing that reads across a bay or storey.

## Replacement direction

- Plain raw/limewashed daub infill forms the main wall surface. Wattle is the concealed backing,
  rather than a visible stick lattice on every finished panel.
- Principal posts, including one continuous three-dimensional corner post, define the structure.
  The same member must read on both elevations. Inside corners, wall ends and junctions need explicit geometry.
- Continuous sill, floor and head rails appear at player-chosen heights. Do not add a rail to every block edge.
- Optional narrower studs provide close-studded facades; wider bays remain possible.
- Left/right single diagonal modules form intentional braces over larger bays. X-bracing is an optional pattern,
  rather than the default fill for every square.
- Keep ordinary placeable blocks. Use a small set of explicit framing/infill modules with automatic joins
  between matching edges; do not make the game guess floor boundaries or brace layouts.
- Keep wall thickness consistent, plaster close to the timber face and grain aligned with each timber.
  Use beam-like wood faces rather than plank-board faces. Retain the six-wood palette, with spruce/dark oak
  providing the strongest starting match to the dark timber in the references.

## First visual acceptance example

Build a two-storey corner example with a masonry ground floor, a three-block-high framed upper storey,
two- to three-block structural bays, sparse diagonal bracing, window openings and a framed gable.
Show both elevations and a close view of the corner. The corner must have one post, continuous rails,
no doubled members, no floating trim and no open seams. Compare broad-bay and close-studded sections.
Geometry and collision must agree in every facing; drops, rotations and save/reload must retain identities.

The wider Rye village direction also calls for jetties, suitable windows, brick/stone lower floors,
tile-hung walls, clay roof tiles and varied adjoining facades. Those are follow-on building families;
they should not delay reviewing the revised wall construction.

The subsequent jetty investigation is recorded in [Jettied storeys and half-block wall placement](JETTIED-STOREYS-DIRECTION.md).
It describes partial walls, matching returns/corners and the timber floor/underside system implemented in 0.1.2.

The existing candidate bundle remains the record of the initial implementation. A replacement needs
fresh screenshots and its own qualification evidence; do not overwrite the old frozen artifacts or worlds.
